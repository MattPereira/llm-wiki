# Social Media Performance Metrics

*From YTD to MTD, and soon WTD and DTD? It seems that everything matters, except the long term. *

**TheBigBerbowski Investing** · 2026-08-30 · [read](https://thebigberbowski.substack.com/p/social-media-performance-metrics)

I noticed that people on social media frequently brag about their investment performance, posting seemingly unreal returns ranging from 300% to 500%, and sometimes even 1,000%+.

However, very few people post their performance from one or two years ago. This is likely because they either ended up underperforming or they simply were not invested at the time. On top of the YTD groups, MTD groups have emerged lately. In these spaces, not only is there an expectation of a 300% YTD return, but members are also expected to generate at least 100% to 200% MTD.

Then, there is the other side of the story.

Today’s online crowd not only ridicules legendary investors like Warren Buffett, but they also post comparisons of their own short-term performance against some of the greatest fund managers of all time.

When seeing someone achieve a 5% return, the initial impression should not be that they are bad investors or underperforming. There are several crucial points to consider:

- They could be managing millions of dollars and are uncomfortable chasing high-risk assets.
- They might be experts in specific industries currently going through a cyclical downturn.
- They could be long-term investors whose strategies underperform in volatile, short-term markets.

Furthermore, what does YTD return actually tell us about long-term performance? I believe an investor needs to perform consistently across a 5 to 10 year period before their track record can be accurately judged.

Someone just starting out could simply get lucky and jump 300% on a poor quality stock, while an unlucky investor could be down 20% on a fundamentally solid company due to short term market noise.

To summarize: never be discouraged by a 20% to 30% annual return.

Sustaining that level of performance over the long run is guaranteed to completely transform your financial life.

To put this into perspective, an initial investment of $10,000 compounding at 30% annually grows to $137,858.49 after 10 years. If you stretch that horizon to 20 years, that same $10,000 becomes $1,900,496.38.

If you think that’s too long, I have to remind you of my favorite quote from Lynch which is: “People don’t want to get rich slow, so they get poor quick.”

Stay patient. Don’t feel bad about having “only” 20, 30 or 50% annually.

You’re doing great.
