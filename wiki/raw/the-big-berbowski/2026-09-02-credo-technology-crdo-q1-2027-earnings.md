# Credo Technology $CRDO: Q1 2027 Earnings Review

Credo reported record financial results for its fiscal Q1 2027, massively exceeding both internal guidance and consensus estimates. Revenue reached an all-time high of $479.0 million (up 114.7% YoY and 9.6% QoQ), while non-GAAP diluted EPS reached $1.20, outperforming the $1.16 Street consensus. Furthermore, management confidently raised its full-year fiscal 2027 revenue guidance from 'greater than 80%' to 'greater than 85% year-over-year'. Despite this flawless fundamental execution, CRDO shares experienced a sharp post-market contraction, tumbling 8.65% in regular trading to close at $206.63, and sliding another 10.13% in after-hours trading to $185.00.

This report provides a deep analysis of Q1 results and validates the long-term investment thesis.

---

### FISCAL Q4 2026 RECAP

In the previous quarter (Q4 FY2026 ended May 2, 2026), Credo established a robust baseline. The company delivered revenue of $437.0 million (up 157% YoY) and non-GAAP EPS of $1.16, beating estimates of $432.0M and $1.02. Gross margins came in strong at 68.3% (Non-GAAP), and the business generated $226.7 million in non-GAAP net income.

However, the Q4 print was characterized as a 'narrower beat' compared to prior quarters (exceeding guidance midpoint by only $7 million), which triggered a mild margin scare.

### Q1 FY2027 PERFORMANCE VS. GUIDANCE & STREET ESTIMATES

Credo's performance in Q1 FY2027 represents a 'double beat'. The company surpassed the high end of its internal revenue guidance range by $4.0 million and outperformed consensus Street expectations.

**The GAAP vs. Non-GAAP Margin difference:**

The primary catalyst for short-term sentiment deceleration was the sequential drop in GAAP gross margin to 64.5% from 68.2% in Q4 FY26. However, this compression was entirely non-operational. As shown in the 8-K reconciliation tables, the closing of the DustPhotonics transaction introduced a $11.0 million amortization of acquired intangible assets and $5.7 million in stock-based compensation within cost of revenue. Adjusted for these non-cash expenses, the core Non-GAAP gross margin was stable at 68.0%, reflecting highly resilient unit economics and robust pricing power.

### Q2 FY2027 OUTLOOK & EXPANDED FULL-YEAR GUIDANCE

Credo provided extremely bullish forward looking expectations for the upcoming fiscal Q2 ending October 31, 2026. The company guided Q2 revenue to $525.0 million – $535.0 million, representing 10.6% QoQ growth and crushing the Street consensus of $520.0 million. Non-GAAP gross margin is guided to 67.0% – 69.0% (68% midpoint), confirming stable unit economics. Q2 Non-GAAP operating expenses are guided to $100.0M – $105.0M.

**FULL-YEAR FY2027 GUIDANCE RAISE:**

Management formally raised its full-year total revenue growth expectation from 'greater than 80%' to 'greater than 85% year-over-year'. This translates to full-year revenue exceeding $2.47 billion. Furthermore, management expects its Non-GAAP net margin to remain near 50% for the full year, resulting in expected full-year adjusted net income of approximately $1.23 billion.

### BALANCE SHEET & 8K

Analyzing the Q1 FY2027 balance sheet reveals major capital allocation shifts following the DustPhotonics closing on May 28, 2026:

- Cash and Liquidity: Total cash and short-term investments stood at $764.3 million (cash of $466.9M and short-term investments of $297.4M), down sequentially from $1.44 billion. This drop is directly attributed to the $750.0 million upfront cash payment for the DustPhotonics transaction.
- Goodwill & Intangible Assets: Goodwill skyrocketed from $92.8 million to $986.4 million, and net Intangible Assets jumped from $29.3 million to $378.8 million, collectively representing $1.365 billion (up $1.24B QoQ). This represents the acquired technological IP (Carmel PIC platform) and goodwill associated with the transaction.
- Strategic Inventory Buffer: Inventory rose sequentially by $62.2 million to $313.1 million. CFO Dan Fleming confirmed this was a deliberate choice: 'Inventory levels increased... reflecting supply chain investments'. This mitigates foundry and capacity constraints ahead of the second-half volume ramp.

### THESIS CHECK (VS EXPECTATIONS IN THE DEEP DIVE)

**$600M+ Optical Revenue Target in FY27**: Credo guided above $600M of optical revenue across ZF Optics, SiPho PICs, and optical DSPs, with each contributing $100M+.

On the call, CFO Dan Fleming reiterated this exact breakdown: “We continue to expect an inflection in the second half, driven by more than $600 million in optical revenue with ZeroFlap Optics, silicon photonics, PICs, and optical DSPs, each contributing more than $100 million.”

Furthermore, CEO Bill Brennan highlighted that ZeroFlap Optics is experiencing active customer ramps across both hyperscalers and neo-clouds, positioning it as the leading near-term volume contributor.

**50/50 Copper/Optical Mix & Large Optical TAM**: thesis highlights a 50/50 long-term mix and the potential for optical to exceed copper.

CEO Bill Brennan reiterated this long-term balance during Q&A: 'Long term, I think you'll see AECs grow, and as we continue to scale as a company, I think you'll see a really nice balance between copper solutions and optical.' Optics is growing faster simply because it starts from a smaller base. The addressable pluggable optical transceiver market is projected by forecasters to grow from 60 million units in 2026 to 175 million units by 2030, presenting an enormous TAM.

**SiPho PIC Traction & DustPhotonics** In Q1, Credo recognized its first-ever silicon photonics PIC revenue. More importantly, Brennan announced: 'We have got design wins with two major players for next generation ramps that will occur in FY 2028 and maybe starting in the late part of this fiscal year.' Critically, these first two wins do not include Credo DSPs, meaning Credo sold standalone PICs. This leaves a massive up-sell pathway once Credo begins bundling its DSPs and PICs together

**Co-Packaged Optics (CPO) and Near-Port Optics (NPO) ahead of the FY2028 ramp.** On the call, Brennan provided major updates on this front. Credo officially joined the Open CPX MSA consortium specifically to address the NPO scale-up interconnect market. Brennan said: 'We are seeing increasing customer activity around NPO for scale-up networks with confirmed design wins expected to begin ramping in our fiscal 2028... we'll lead with the silicon photonics PIC, but we'll also look at doing the complete optical engine long term as we add more functionality to our portfolio.' This establishes a firm operational pathway to monetization in scale-up GPU fabrics.

**Is the H2 Inflection on Track?**

A key concern for bears has been the heavily back-end-loaded nature of Credo's FY2027 revenue targets. In Q4, management guided to 'mid-single-digit percentage' sequential growth in H1. However, Q1 delivered 9.61% sequential growth, and the Q2 midpoint of $530M implies 10.65% sequential growth. This acceleration proves that H1 is already tracking significantly ahead of the historical run-rate, which materially de-risks the H2 inflection. To hit its raised full-year target of >85% growth ($2.47B), Credo needs to average roughly $730 million per quarter in H2. This will be supported by production ramps of ZeroFlap Optics, Blue Heron 200G/lane retimers, and early 1.6T optical DSP shipments.

### DUSTPHOTONICS INTEGRATION

The acquisition of DustPhotonics represents a shift in Credo's competitive positioning. Before this deal, Credo was a high-performance analog silicon vendor. Now, by integrating DustPhotonics' Silicon Photonics (SiPho) PIC technology with its own DSP and SerDes IP, Credo is the only fabless player that owns the entire high-speed optical connectivity stack:

SerDes IP ➔ Optical DSP ➔ Silicon Photonics PIC ➔ Pluggable Transceiver (ZeroFlap) ➔ PILOT Software

This full-stack vertical integration provides three distinct structural advantages:

1. Cost Leadership: Eliminates margin stacking by cutting out third-party optical component providers. As port speeds transition from 800G to 1.6T and 3.2T, silicon photonics packaging yields become the dominant cost factor. Owning the PIC layer allows Credo to optimize manufacturing yields directly, lowering costs at volume.
2. Telemetry and the Reliability Moat: By optimizing the DSP and PIC together, Credo can light up 'continuous telemetry' via its PILOT software platform. Brennan highlighted that PILOT monitors critical physical-layer metrics (eye height, SNR, post-FEC histograms) across all six segments of an optical link. This acts as a 'check engine light' for AI cluster operators, identifying fiber degradation, dust, or electrostatic damage before a link flap occurs, preventing expensive GPU idle time.
3. Strategic Up-sell: Standard transceivers compete in a crowded merchant market. By offering ZeroFlap Optics, which integrates hardware and telemetry SDKs directly into switch software, Credo creates a high-margin, sticky solution that rivals cannot provide.

### FY2028 EMERGING PRODUCTS & TAM EXPANSION

While the optical ramp defines the FY2027 narrative, the earnings call highlighted three massive product pillars planned for production revenue in FY 2028, representing significant underappreciated upside for the bull thesis:

- OmniConnect AI Memory & 'Weaver' Gearbox: Designed to address the 'memory wall' and memory fanout bottlenecks in large-scale AI inference clusters. Bill Brennan revealed: 'We believe OmniConnect solutions can represent thousands of dollars of Credo content per GPU with revenue beginning in fiscal 2028.' Brennan confirmed their first customer, Positron, is deploying a 2TB Weaver-enabled system.
- Active LED Cables (ALCs): Utilizing microLED emitters, ALCs extend the reliability and power benefits of active copper cables to reaches of up to 30 meters. This bridges the gap between active copper (AECs, up to 7m) and traditional laser-based optics, creating a third differentiated product category. Real-world demonstrations will occur at OFC in October, with production scheduled for FY28.
- Blue Heron 200G/Lane & 1.6T AECs: The transition to 200G/lane architectures is a major growth vector for copper. Brennan confirmed that 1.6T AEC deployments will begin contributing in late FY27 and ramp in a much larger way in FY28, with 200G-per-lane serving as the volume crossover point during the fiscal year
