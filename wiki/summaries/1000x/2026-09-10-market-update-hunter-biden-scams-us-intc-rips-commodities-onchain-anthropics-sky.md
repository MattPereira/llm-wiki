## Thesis

The easy crypto trade is in peril: BTC has stalled under 80K while flows rotate
back into AI hardware. Trim crypto, rotate to memory/AI equities, and treat
on-chain perp venues (Hyperliquid, Lighter) as the structural long — they, not
ICE or NYMEX, are where new tradable markets now get created.

  **below 70K "quite high" probability**. Plans to re-buy sub-70. Also floats the

  100–200%/yr; patience and crystallizing gains beat waiting for a 1000x.

  Bitcoin caution.
- **Memory caveat:** physical memory is cooling as consumers balk at prices and
  wait for supply; the bottleneck rotates, so this isn't set-and-forget.

## Commodities on chain

The long argument of the episode, between Jonah (ex-commodities) and Avi.

- **Jonah's case:** oil benchmarks are set by a handful of traders in a 30-minute
  London window on 1970s technology. WTI and Brent together represent ~20bps of
  global physical consumption yet price the world. Nigerian, Malaysian, Saudi
  differentials should settle transparently on chain; an **AMM is a better
  settlement mechanism for a differential** than a phone call and a fax machine.
- **ICE and NYMEX can't innovate** — bureaucracies riding decades-old benchmarks,
  with gazillion-dollar minimums and mountains of paperwork. The most innovative
  commodities listing venue in recent memory was **FTX** (live lumber trading
  during the crisis) before Sam took it down.
- **Avi's pushback:** retail speculates where it believes it has edge from public
  information. Nobody punts a Nigerian crude differential because they know
  someone smarter will take the other side. Brent works because you can have a
  view on Iran. So crypto brings liquidity to *big* commodities, not niche ones.
- **Jonah's resolution:** light the fire with kindling (retail perps on, say, jet
  fuel), then hedgers and speculators arrive and the institutional logs pile on.
  Dated futures and swaps still matter for producers — a perp can't lock in a
  price against an August harvest, and oil producers hedge with daily-settling
  swaps.
- **Avi's steelman for niche markets:** market access lets domain insiders express
  views they currently can't — his example is a Lockheed Skunk Works procurement
  employee who knows component demand is about to spike, with no insider-trading
  regime covering that commodity. New information gets a place to go, which is
  what justifies new products.
- Evidence it's already happening: the viral Coinalyze chart showing altcoin open
  interest exceeding Bitcoin's — usually read as a top signal — was miscounted.
  The "other" bucket **included equities being traded on chain** via Lighter and
  Hyperliquid. The rotation was into on-chain equities, not alt speculation.

## "Getting people invested is a national security issue"

- Wages are no longer the channel through which corporate profits reach people —
  companies now earn far more than labor's marginal contribution, so pay can't
  keep up. **The only way in is ownership.**
- Reads **Trump accounts** ($1,000 into the S&P per newborn) as a policy
  telegraph that *cash is trash*; ~10%/yr in the S&P is roughly what's needed to
  hold purchasing power, and the CPI print doesn't reflect that.
- Wants every US public company to run Capital One-style discounted employee
  stock purchase plans (15% discount, one-year lockup) for alignment.
- The charitable case for memecoins: for some buyers it's their **first-ever
  financial investment** (per Se of FOMO). Losing money on one more likely
  redirects them into safer investing than out of markets entirely.
- Underlying driver of the speculation urge: fear of AI, fear of stagnation, and
  a desire to escape a perceived permanent underclass — a fear Avi thinks is
  mistaken on wages but self-fulfilling in behavior.

## "Never been easier to be a quant"

- Back-testing with AI is cheap enough that anyone can find a rules-based,
  decorrelated strategy. Explicit examples: buy any Kalshi sports contract
  trading at an X% discount to Vegas odds, at scale and diversified; or profile a
  memecoin's hourly price history, long the strong hours, short the weak ones,
  with **$20 for a month**, then re-up if it works.
- Automate via agent scaffolding (Astra, Hermes, OpenRouter) or 1000x's own
  terminal; pair trades via pair protocol.
- Hard requirement: **a process**. "Throwing darts with a blindfold on" doesn't work.

## AGI and the melt-up

Closing argument, and the source of the title. Jonah: Astra collapses a 48-hour
Ableton production job to about an hour *with* real control over the knobs, and
that compression across every discipline implies parabolic acceleration — 8
billion agents who have been communicating at dial-up speeds suddenly get a T1
line. Conclusion: bullish everything that passes value back to stakeholders
(Hyperliquid buybacks, equities) except bonds.

Sits alongside genuine unease — "this technology is too real, it may end up
destroying us as a species" — but the trade until then is the melt-up.
