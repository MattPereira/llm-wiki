# Sandisk $SNDK: Investor Day 2026 Summary

*Rewriting the NAND Business Model*

**TheBigBerbowski Investing** · 2026-08-19 · [read](https://thebigberbowski.substack.com/p/sandisk-sndk-investor-day-2026-summary)

A couple of days ago we had an opportunity to listen to a lengthy webcast of Sandisk’s Investor Day. Sandisk stock started recovering and accelerated to 40%+ return in the last five days.

Is Sandisk back in play? Let’s dig in to see what they showed to shareholders during the investor day.

![](https://substackcdn.com/image/fetch/$s_!gOTl!,f_auto,q_auto:good,fl_progressive:steep/https%3A%2F%2Fsubstack-post-media.s3.amazonaws.com%2Fpublic%2Fimages%2F8b7d225c-b6ec-48b5-b90b-ad30707ede9c_1660x911.png)

> “You are going to make 10-year investments and then you are going to hold a quarterly auction to see who buys your stuff and at what price. It just makes no sense. I think we are rapidly transitioning to something that is very different.” - David Goeckeler, Chairman & CEO of Sandisk

That quote probably explains Sandisk’s 2026 Investor Day better than any slide in the presentation.

Sandisk’s Investor Day was much more ambitious than a conventional semiconductor product update. Management was asking investors to reconsider what kind of company Sandisk is today.

Historically, as you probably heard many times by now, NAND flash has been one of the more cyclical parts of the semiconductor industry. Manufacturers increase capacity when demand and pricing are strong, and then when the supply eventually catches up, prices fall, margins compress, capital spending gets cut, and the cycle begins again. This cyclicality has made memory/storage companies very difficult to value through normal earnings multiples because earnings at the top and the bottom of the cycle are dramatically different.

Sandisk Investor Day aimed to show several structural changes that their business is going through.

AI build out is creating a new source of large-scale NAND demand across the globe. Today, Sandisk is moving a large piece of its business into multi year agreements with customers. On top of that technological improvements are allowing the company to grow the number of bits produced without requiring a proportional increase in manufacturing capacity.

Management believes these changes can produce something the NAND industry has historically struggled to deliver: sustained growth, high profitability and stable FCF.

## 2026 has already transformed the earnings profile

The starting point for this Investor Day is management’s showcase of what happened during FY2026.

Sandisk reported approximately $20.2B of full year revenue, representing roughly 175% YoY growth. Full-year non-GAAP gross margin reached 71.6%, compared with 30.3% in the previous year, while adjusted FCF reached $8.7B. By the Q4, annualized revenue was running at roughly $36B and annualized adjusted FCF at approximately $20 billion.

Those are extraordinary numbers for a NAND manufacturer.

![](https://substackcdn.com/image/fetch/$s_!gEBk!,f_auto,q_auto:good,fl_progressive:steep/https%3A%2F%2Fsubstack-post-media.s3.amazonaws.com%2Fpublic%2Fimages%2F7bb66b33-47a3-4db0-baa6-dbded07bbbd0_1661x912.png)

I’m sure a lot of you are wondering if 2026 represents an unusually strong memory cycle or the beginning of something more durable. The Investor Day was trying to make the latter case.

Sandisk’s long term framework relies on three main goals:

- increasing profitability
- reducing cyclicality
- generating consistent revenue growth

Instead of isolating these goals, management believes that technology, customer contracts and capital efficiency reinforce each other.

## The business model

Sandisk calls its new contractual structure the New Business Model (NBM). The basic idea is this: instead of renegotiating most of its business every quarter, Sandisk is moving a large portion of its supply into multi-year agreements with their key customers.

CFO Luis Visoso described why the old model was problematic:

> “We are moving from a quarterly price negotiation to large multi-year engagement... Over that time, when we were operating in that model, we practically generated no shareholder value. Made capital investments super difficult because they were very risky.”

That is arguably the core of the entire Investor Day. Sandisk is a company which makes manufacturing decisions years in advance, but historically it had very little visibility into the economics attached to it.

Management said the average NBM contract now lasts more than four years, with the longest agreement at five years. They expect about half of FY2027 bits and about 2/3 of FY2028 bits to be covered under NBMs.

Sandisk disclosed $93.9B of total contract value across eight NBM customers at floor pricing, with $91.1 billion of remaining performance obligations. Three of those eight customers are U.S. hyperscalers, according to management.

![](https://substackcdn.com/image/fetch/$s_!DwUt!,f_auto,q_auto:good,fl_progressive:steep/https%3A%2F%2Fsubstack-post-media.s3.amazonaws.com%2Fpublic%2Fimages%2F9cc51e40-c9a4-4cce-9b01-ce095a306207_1388x516.png)

They has also secured $16.5 billion of financial guarantees related to these arrangements, primarily through third-party financial institutions. This is how it works: customers secure their long term supply of NAND, Sandisk gets committed volumes, and their pricing is protected by floors, and the guarantees provide another layer of financial protection. For customers, this model offers supply certainty and for Sandisk it creates far greater revenue visibility and downside protection than their old quarter-to-quarter NAND model.

## The most important quote from Investor Day

The obvious bear case is that these contracts sound good today because NAND pricing is strong but what happens when pricing falls? Visoso addressed that:

> “Even at the lowest price, at the floor pricing, at any point in time, we do not expect these NVMs to be below 80%. We expect that to be around 80%... every single year over the period, we should be able to be even at floor pricing around 80%.”

This may be the most important statement from the entire event. Management is basically saying that the contracted part of the business should still generate approximately 80% gross margins even if pricing falls all the way to contractual floors. That is a completely different economic setup from the historical NAND models, where the standard sequence of cyclicality is relatively straightforward.

Sandisk is working hard to break away from that cyclicality. The company is not claiming that NAND prices will never decline again. Goeckeler was actually careful about that detail:

> “We are not saying cyclicality is going away. The whole world is cyclical. The whole business is cyclical. What we are trying to do is get this wild cyclicality of this business, just dampen that down, get more sustained value creation.”

To summarize: the bull case is not that NAND is no longer cyclical, but instead it’s based on the case that Sandisk’s earnings may become much less cyclical than NAND pricing itself.

## Supply discipline

One of the reasons memory industries usually become oversupplied is that the technology creates massive productivity improvements. Every new NAND generation can produce a lot more bits from the same wafer footprint, and those improvements often turned into more output which eventually contributed to oversupply.

Today, Sandisk seems to be aware of that problem. Goeckeler explained it.

> “Through the application of innovation, we can grow output per wafer at a rate of 27% a year. Which, by the way, tells you, you can’t just release nodes whenever they’re available. Otherwise you’re going to flood the market with supply and you’re going to have another ’23 situation.”

That is a significant change in the company’s philosophy. Technology enables roughly 27% annual productivity growth, while management is targeting only mid-to-high-teens bit growth over longer periods. Sandisk does not want or have to translate every available productivity improvement immediately into market supply.

Goeckeler made the point again during Q&A: “We just need to not overrun the market.” This is very important because new contracts alone will not protect industry economics if every NAND producer aggressively expands its capacity.

## More bits without proportionally more capital

Sandisk’s technology roadmap explains one of the most important aspects of Sandisk’s financial targets: low capital intensity. Sandisk’s BiCS8 architecture already provides improvements in density, bandwidth and power efficiency, while BiCS10 pushes those economics further. The company’s 1Tb TLC BiCS10 product is based on 332 layers and management says it improves memory density by 59% versus BiCS8 while also improving interface bandwidth, read/write performance and power efficiency.

Sandisk CTO Alper Ilkbahar also discussed the 2Tb QLC version, saying:

> “Each BiCS10 2 terabit wafer has 65% more bits than the BiCS8 2 terabit.”

What does this mean? It means that Sandisk can increase the amount of storage from its manufacturing footprint without increasing physical wafer capacity at the same rate.

Goeckeler described it this way:

> “Growth is primarily driven in this business by the application of intellectual capital, not financial capital.”

If growth increasingly comes from engineering rather than building new fabs, the conversion of incremental revenue into free cash flow can become very attractive. This helps Sandisk’s long term model enormously.

## Guidance

We were provided with targets for FY2028 through FY2030. Sandisk expects average revenue growth in the mid-to-high teens, non-GAAP gross margin around 80%, non-GAAP operating margin around 75%, adjusted free cash flow margin of approximately 50%, and capital intensity in the mid-single-digit range.

![](https://substackcdn.com/image/fetch/$s_!VpkP!,f_auto,q_auto:good,fl_progressive:steep/https%3A%2F%2Fsubstack-post-media.s3.amazonaws.com%2Fpublic%2Fimages%2Fec12a5d3-2ace-443b-b80b-d550687023b0_1658x910.png)

In relation to guidance, Visoso said:

> “When you aggregate all of that for 2028 through 2030, we expect to grow revenue mid to high teens, consistent with [bit] growth... We expect non-GAAP gross margin to be around 80%... And then you get to 50% adjusted FCF after paying for taxes, working capital, capital spending.”

“Revenue growth consistent with bit growth” matters. If revenue and bit shipments grow at similar rates, Sandisk is not building its FY2028-FY2030 framework around continuous NAND price increases. They are arguing that the business can maintain improved economics even after the current pricing environment normalizes.

## Why AI may make that possible

Sandisk story works with the demand, without it, not so much. This is where AI enters the thesis.

Historically, NAND market relied heavily on consumer electronics like smartphones, pcs, removable storage. Today, the focus is shifting to data centers.

Based on management’s estimates, the NAND industry revenue could exceed $300B in 2026 and approach $500B year later (for context, historical average was close to $60B). These numbers do not represent the industry consensus, but rather management’s own views.

## HBF could move Sandisk closer to the GPU

High Bandwidth Flash (HBF) is Sandisk’s attempt to create NAND architecture specifically suited to AI inference. The idea is not to replace HBM everywhere. HBM provides a lot of bandwidth but relatively limited capacity and comes at a high cost, while HBF is designed to offer dramatically more capacity close to the accelerator while retaining significantly higher bandwidth than traditional storage.

Sandisk has described several potential architectures, including using HBF alongside HBM or creating disaggregated systems where HBM operates as the high-speed tier while HBF holds much larger model weights or KV cache datasets. The most interesting slide from the technology section compared HBF with HBM in an internal AI inference simulation.

Sandisk claims one HBF-equipped GPU could run a workload that required eight HBM-equipped GPUs simply to fit the model, providing an 8x advantage in minimum-system capital efficiency. At higher throughput, four HBF-equipped GPUs generated similar token output to eight HBM-equipped GPUs, implying approximately 2x GPU efficiency.

![](https://substackcdn.com/image/fetch/$s_!TTkx!,f_auto,q_auto:good,fl_progressive:steep/https%3A%2F%2Fsubstack-post-media.s3.amazonaws.com%2Fpublic%2Fimages%2F12ba5d10-5939-443f-91bb-a925091852e8_1665x917.png)

CTO Alper Ilkbahar summarized the result:

> “With four HBF GPUs, we are able to match the performance of eight HBM GPUs.”
>
> Then he added:
>
> “Your capital will go twice as far... This is fundamentally going to change the economics of AI.”

While it definitely looks impressive, it’s important to add that these are Sandisk’s internal simulations. HBF is still a technology in development, but there has been tangible progress. Sandisk says the first HBF memory die has now taped out and first inference product samples are expected in 2027.

![](https://substackcdn.com/image/fetch/$s_!ETnk!,f_auto,q_auto:good,fl_progressive:steep/https%3A%2F%2Fsubstack-post-media.s3.amazonaws.com%2Fpublic%2Fimages%2F4de5ebaa-cded-45a6-bf0e-8d9be15dd548_1659x912.png)

Sandisk is also working to create an industry ecosystem around this technology, including its collaboration with SK hynix and the HBF Technology Consortium.

For Sandisk investors, it’s important to mention that HBF does not need to succeed for Sandisk thesis to work. The near/medium-term argument is NAND demand, NBM contracts and free cash flow. HBF is optionality on top.

If HBF works out, Sandisk potentially moves from being a storage supplier into a more strategically important position within the AI memory hierarchy.

## Capital allocation

Sandisk’s capital allocation plan is very simple. They say they wants to invest in technology and the business, maintain a strong balance sheet with no debt, and return 100% of excess cash to shareholders.

Yes 100% of excess cash to shareholders.

![](https://substackcdn.com/image/fetch/$s_!2KXm!,f_auto,q_auto:good,fl_progressive:steep/https%3A%2F%2Fsubstack-post-media.s3.amazonaws.com%2Fpublic%2Fimages%2F42ef70b6-9da9-4dc9-b2cb-db5f036f37ff_1660x915.png)

> Visoso: “There is no trick. It is 100% of the cash. Excess cash will go back to shareholders.”

Thit commitment from Sandisk is very relevant if the company comes close to its free cash flow targets. A business generating 50% adjusted FCF margins while requiring only mid-single-digit capital intensity would produce massive amounts of distributable cash.

## Is this killing the NAND cycle?

It’s too early to say. These NBM contracts have not been tested through an industry downturn. Historically, memory LTAs have not always been durable as investors expected when supply and demand conditions changed dramatically. Prior memory cycles have seen customers finding ways out of LTAs, while additional industry capacity could begin returning from competitors in 2027.

This risk should not be ignored.

But Sandisk does not need to eliminate cyclicality for their strategy to work. They only need to reduce the damage caused by downturns. Compared with previous cycles, there are now real structural mechanisms attempting to achieve that: multi-year committed demand, pricing floors, financial guarantees, supply discipline, technology-driven bit growth, low capital intensity and a commitment to return excess cash.

This is what makes the current Sandisk story more interesting than simply saying NAND prices are high.

> Goeckeler addressed the skeptics during the Q&A:
>
> “There are the die-hard cycle people... ‘This is the way it has been for 20 years.’ I happen to have a belief you can change the future.”

Maybe. The next downturn will tell us more.
