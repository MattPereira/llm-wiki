# Stock Market Weekly Retrospect #3

**TheBigBerbowski Investing** · 2026-08-29 · [read](https://thebigberbowski.substack.com/p/stock-market-weekly-retrospect-3)

### Iran sanctions

Treasury Secretary Bessent unveiled the sanctions package Monday, targeting five channels - digital assets, technology, gold, aviation, and shipping - and blacklisting more than 60 Iran-linked individuals, vessels, and entities.

On Truth Social, Trump called it “the most crushing economic operation ever taken against any country” and warned countries that keep any economic ties to Iran would face severe consequences. Notably, at the press conference a reporter pointed out this wasn’t really a “D-Day” at all since no sanctions were imposed immediately and Bessent’s response was blunt: “Why would I want to blow up the global financial system?”

![](https://substackcdn.com/image/fetch/$s_!VBZV!,f_auto,q_auto:good,fl_progressive:steep/https%3A%2F%2Fsubstack-post-media.s3.amazonaws.com%2Fpublic%2Fimages%2F93e5593c-6f7b-4f84-9611-af4c381e601d_480x361.webp)

Iran escalated to formal diplomatic channels. Araghchi sent a letter to UN António Guterres calling the sanctions “economic terrorism” and asking the UN to reject and refuse to implement them. He also raised humanitarian concerns, arguing the measures could restrict access to food, medicine, and medical equipment for ordinary Iranians.

On Friday,Iran’s Foreign Ministry publicly urged its trading partners not to comply with the sanctions, calling them “illegal and unjustified.” But the same day, Araghchi struck a bit different tone in a separate comment, saying diplomacy with the US was “not impossible” if Washington understood that “pressure doesn’t work”.

### Canada tariffs

Trump threatened 50% tariffs on Canadian cars, trucks, auto parts, and steel starting Jan. 1, 2027, writing on Truth Social “Canada has been ripping off the United States of America for years.” This came after Canada’s PM Mark Carney vowed to retaliate “dollar for dollar,” and after the US had already imposed 50% tariffs on about $20 billion of Canadian goods (wine, cement, hockey sticks) that Saturday when trade talks collapsed.

![](https://substackcdn.com/image/fetch/$s_!I62z!,f_auto,q_auto:good,fl_progressive:steep/https%3A%2F%2Fsubstack-post-media.s3.amazonaws.com%2Fpublic%2Fimages%2Fe33a904f-9059-46a3-bb99-1d0cdeb12b8a_447x447.jpeg)

On Tuesday, Canada announced retaliatory tariffs on roughly $20 billion of US goods, hitting steel, dairy, and other categories, in response to Monday's US tariff moves.

The Conference Board's Consumer Confidence Index — a widely watched monthly gauge of how Americans feel about the economy — fell to 89.4 in August, down from 90.2 in July. It's the index's second straight monthly decline and its weakest reading since January.

### **PCE Inflation, July data**

Headline PCE: +0.2% for the month, +3.7% YoY — both above the 0.1%/3.6% economists expected.

Core PCE: +0.2% monthly, holding at 3.3% annually — unchanged from June, meaning the disinflation trend has stalled rather than reversed.

Both readings sit well above the Fed’s 2% target, with little forward momentum toward it.

### **Venezuela oil deal**

Trump posted on Truth Social: “The United States of America has just entered into an Agreement with the Country of Venezuela on, THE BIGGEST OIL DEAL IN WORLD HISTORY!” He said it gives the US majority control of more than 65 billion barrels of Venezuela’s proven reserves, negotiated by Secretary of State Marco Rubio, Defense Secretary Hegseth, and Venezuela’s acting President Delcy Rodríguez. Context: this comes as US gas prices hit about $4.09/gallon (up 27% year-over-year) and the Strategic Petroleum Reserve fell below 300 million barrels - its lowest level in decades - partly due to the Iran war disrupting the Strait of Hormuz.

### **Fed Chair Kevin Warsh’s Jackson Hole speech**

He said directly: “While this summer’s PCE and CPI readings were better than expected, they do not tell me that underlying trends have meaningfully improved.”

Markets reacted immediately - odds of a September rate hike (priced via CME FedWatch-style futures) jumped to around 50–60%, up from roughly 35% before the speech.

The market’s reaction was immediate and connected across assets:

- Rate expectations: odds of a September hike jumped from about 35% (Thursday) to 50–58% (Friday) on CME FedWatch-style pricing.
- Treasury yields & dollar: yields rose and the dollar strengthened roughly 1% on the day.
- Gold: fell as much as 3.2–3.4%, breaking below $4,500 for the first time in about a week, as the stronger dollar and higher real yields made a non-yielding asset less attractive.
- Bitcoin: dropped roughly 3%, pulling back from its attempted breakout above $80,000–81,500.
- Equities: small caps (Russell 2000) were hit hardest, down ~1.4% Friday, more sensitive to rate expectations than mega-cap tech; the S&P and Nasdaq fell more modestly since Nvidia’s earnings glow was still providing some support.

### Chinese bots

X's Safety team identified roughly 200,000 accounts tied to a suspected Chinese bot farm, with about 200 of them found actively posting content designed to sway American opinion against AI data centers - including material on grid strain, rising utility prices, and cartoons portraying data-center operators as profiting at the public's expense.

The concern: this looks like an attempt by a foreign adversary to slow down U.S. AI development amid the broader US-China AI race. Congressional Republicans have already been probing similar influence campaigns - a House Energy and Commerce Committee letter in June flagged China, Russia, and Iran as suspects, citing one estimate that opposition campaigns have delayed or blocked about $45.8 billion in data center projects in Virginia alone. Separately, OpenAI said in June it had disrupted two Chinese-linked covert networks using ChatGPT to generate fake content on the same topic, though those campaigns reportedly didn't gain much traction.

### Moving Updates from Hot Chips:

Hot chips is usually a boring, geeky conference where electronics afficionados head to talk shop. This year though, it had much of fanfare. Many moving updates came from the conference on how future of Memory and Optical communication will look like in the next leg of AI development. Let’s see some on high level

#### Memory:

Memory is the defining constraint right now in AI build out. HBM bandwidth is scaling >2x every 2 years against GPU bandwidth scaling 3x in same 2 years creating “Memory wall”. Two main approaches were touched upon in Hot chips

- **zHBM:** Samsung’s approach towards solving the problem is to stack DRAM on top of xPU. Completely eliminates the need for 2D interposer targeting a ~70% I/O power reduction through wafer-on-wafer and hybrid bonding. Likely with HBM5/5e in 2030. This model eases power delivery but has thermal constraints.

![](https://substackcdn.com/image/fetch/$s_!XU0d!,f_auto,q_auto:good,fl_progressive:steep/https%3A%2F%2Fsubstack-post-media.s3.amazonaws.com%2Fpublic%2Fimages%2F61405666-7dc6-4a9d-b618-8c4e0377bed5_1023x920.png)

- **Raptor’s 3D Dram:**D-matrix unveiled another solution for the memory wall problem - Login on DRAM. Single-die accelerators are put on top of vertically stacked DRAMs facing the cold plate that improves thermal performance. It offers SRAM-class bandwidth, HBM-class capacity at 10% of HBM energy. For given power level, they offer 10x bandwidth as current HBMs. This model needs engineering to find a workaround for delivering power through DRAM.

![](https://substackcdn.com/image/fetch/$s_!_1E2!,f_auto,q_auto:good,fl_progressive:steep/https%3A%2F%2Fsubstack-post-media.s3.amazonaws.com%2Fpublic%2Fimages%2F7b7e24e1-199a-4b25-b3c1-f18d10e5ea82_1152x586.png)

- **NAND:**Interestingly enough HBF again was pushed as one possible solution. Samsung also announced zNAND-O. 4-8 NAND wafers are bonded directly on top of logic die to shrink latency and improve bandwidth for edge AI use cases with real-time inference.

One other general theme in all this how hybrid bonding becomes an essential aspect to make that next jump in 3D memory. This can be another lever which can push investments resulting in tailwind for WFEs like Lam research, AMAT.

The benefits of it best illustrated from SK hynix presentation in the event below. Hybrid bonding not only provides thicker core die but also narrows the TSV pitch. This results in improved thermal efficiency (resistance reduction).

![](https://substackcdn.com/image/fetch/$s_!4Nnq!,f_auto,q_auto:good,fl_progressive:steep/https%3A%2F%2Fsubstack-post-media.s3.amazonaws.com%2Fpublic%2Fimages%2F55dadb8f-6de5-47b7-ab7b-5ef0b60d969a_1719x660.png)

#### Optical Communication:

- **Purpose-built Silicon:**As developments progress, one thing is getting obvious: Wide range of use cases like agentic AI models, pre-training, sampling have unique demands requiring unique specifications. For example, Training and Inference have different compute, memory and latency requirements. These are showing up on various architecture emerging. Purpose-built accelerators seem to be the next trend to watch closely.
- **Redefinition of “Networking”:**AI factories used to be built around a homogenous networking principle. Moving away from this seem inevitable. Now, each AI factory seem to have a combination of approaches. For example NVIDIA announced that AI factories need purpose build networks with a combination of scale-up, scale-in, scale-across, context scale and scale-out networks.

![](https://substackcdn.com/image/fetch/$s_!mXaI!,f_auto,q_auto:good,fl_progressive:steep/https%3A%2F%2Fsubstack-post-media.s3.amazonaws.com%2Fpublic%2Fimages%2Fce9754a9-f758-4de3-ac23-bbe33f0e07c5_1595x882.png)

- **Expanding Scale-out and Scale-Across:** Google shared its Virgo network in which 134000 TPUs at 48 petabit per second were in a single cluster spread across multiple data center sites. Buildings will require substantial switching and optical infrastructure.

![](https://substackcdn.com/image/fetch/$s_!DZOW!,f_auto,q_auto:good,fl_progressive:steep/https%3A%2F%2Fsubstack-post-media.s3.amazonaws.com%2Fpublic%2Fimages%2F4f1d77ca-e911-405e-b425-c7b94653151b_1600x769.png)

### World Humanoid Robot Games

China’s five-day World Humanoid Robot Games highlighted just how quickly the country is advancing in robotics. More than 2,000 humanoids from 660+ mostly Chinese companies participated, reinforcing China’s position as the current manufacturing leader; it produced roughly 12,800 humanoids last year, around 90% of global output.

The hardware progress was particularly striking. The fastest robot completed the 100m more than twice as fast as last year, while the best standing jump improved from 0.96m to 3.40m, helped by advances in motors, torque and liquid cooling.

![](https://substackcdn.com/image/fetch/$s_!SL7Q!,f_auto,q_auto:good,fl_progressive:steep/https%3A%2F%2Fsubstack-post-media.s3.amazonaws.com%2Fpublic%2Fimages%2Fcb3e7a02-1f86-46ee-b377-19e1981bd8e2_480x384.jpeg)

The main limitation is still intelligence rather than physical capability. Current robots can become very good at individual tasks such as running or boxing, but struggle to generalize across unfamiliar real-world situations. Unitree founder Wang Xingxing said humanoids are “not yet capable enough for mass deployment”, with AI models for decision-making and interaction remaining the key bottleneck.

So the takeaway is that China is making very rapid progress in humanoid hardware and manufacturing scale, but autonomy, dexterity and genuinely useful general-purpose robots remain significantly behind. The industry’s eventual “ChatGPT moment” for humanoids may therefore still be several years away.

### What FinTwit was actually saying this week

**Data Centers!**

Gavin Baker fired off a blunt declaration: any politician standing against data centers has no business holding office. He called the facilities a revival engine for small-town America, a rare win for blue-collar workers, a force that actually lowers household power bills, and a quiet helper for cleaner energy that barely sips water. Baker then spent the afternoon stacking receipts—utility filings from Georgia, California, Indiana-Michigan, and Louisiana where regulators were being asked to cut residential rates precisely because data-center customers were covering more of the system costs.
