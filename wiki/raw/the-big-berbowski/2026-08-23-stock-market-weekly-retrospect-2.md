### Macro, Bonds, Debt

US national debt officially crossed $40 trillion - the headline number everyone’s anchoring to this week. Trump’s response leans on growth as the fix rather than tax hikes.

![](https://substackcdn.com/image/fetch/$s_!TEmQ!,f_auto,q_auto:good,fl_progressive:steep/https%3A%2F%2Fsubstack-post-media.s3.amazonaws.com%2Fpublic%2Fimages%2Fc40d7465-6721-42f5-afeb-a84ce548cfb2_1280x853.png)

The Treasury has also stepped up its bond buybacks with repurchasing some of its own previously issued debt before maturity. Normally this is routine housekeeping, as they want to keep the bond market liquid by cleaning up older, thinly-traded (”off-the-run”) issues. But reports this week say the program was increased, possibly doubled, and some are reading that less as maintenance and more as a cushion. This could be a sign that the Treasury is worried about liquidity or demand heading into upcoming auctions.

The pressure behind all of this is simple: debt doesn’t just sit there, it has to be rolled over. When existing government debt matures, Treasury doesn’t pay it off in cash - it issues new debt to replace it. With yields elevated, refinancing a large slice of the roughly $40 trillion pile at today’s rates means interest costs eat a growing share of the federal budget.

### $3 trillion in hidden AI liabilities

The WSJ dropped a story this week that deserves more attention than it’s gotten: nine of the biggest US tech companies — Alphabet, Meta, Microsoft, Amazon, Oracle, Nvidia, Broadcom, SpaceX, and AMD — have accumulated roughly $3 trillion in AI-infrastructure liabilities that don’t show up on their balance sheets, per WSJ’s analysis of their latest filings ([full recap here](https://en.oninvest.com/article/tech-giants-have-hidden-3-trillion-in-ai-liabilities-off-their-balance-sheets-wsj)). That’s already about three times these companies’ official on-balance-sheet debt, and it’s growing faster than reported capex.

### Data center bans keep spreading

Texas remains the headline case, but it’s part of a much bigger national wave this week. Recap:

- **Texas**: Governor Abbott’s early-August directive ordering PUCT and ERCOT to audit every data center in the interconnection queue before approving new ones is still in effect and was reinforced this week
- **Wisconsin**: data center backlash is now an active campaign issue — GOP gubernatorial candidate Tom Tiffany is running ads branding his Democratic opponent “Data Center David Crowley”
- **The bigger picture**: a Governing piece this week (Aug 20) notes Maryland alone now has 13 county-level moratoriums - more than half its counties — and that opposition is genuinely bipartisan: Chicago’s progressive mayor and Texas’s Republican governor both called for a pause within weeks of each other. New York became the first state to impose a moratorium via executive order in July; Pennsylvania’s Shapiro signed an order requiring local approval before state permits; Louisville and Fort Worth both passed local moratoriums in the past two weeks.

### Six chip headlines from the week, and what they mean

- Broadcom is in talks to raise $60–80B in debt (some reports say the total package could hit $100B) to fund AI chip capacity for Anthropic and others, via a special-purpose vehicle (SPV) — a separate legal entity that buys the chips and leases them to Anthropic, keeping the debt off both Broadcom’s and Anthropic’s balance sheets.
- Samsung’s board approved returning up to ~110 trillion won ($79.5B) to shareholders in 2026 — the largest payout in Korean corporate history, about five times its prior record — funded by the AI-driven memory boom (Q2 chip profit surged more than 250x YoY).
- Nvidia pushed back hard on a report claiming it planned to ship a China-specific “LPU” (a chip type built on licensed Groq tech) by year-end, saying flatly it has no such product on its roadmap. This lands just before Nvidia’s Aug 26 earnings.
- Japan’s trade ministry (METI) is requesting another ¥150B (~$944M) for state-backed chipmaker Rapidus in the FY2027 budget, bringing total government backing toward ¥400B+ (with total planned support closer to ¥3T over time), as Rapidus works toward 2nm mass production by 2027. Japan continues doubling down on Rapidus as a strategic bet to reduce reliance on TSMC, even though Rapidus remains roughly two years behind TSMC and Samsung on the same node.
- Zhongji Innolight posted H1 2026 net profit up 241.7% YoY to 13.65 billion yuan, on revenue up 182.5% to 41.78 billion yuan — driven by 800G/1.6T transceiver demand from global hyperscalers, with overseas revenue up 210%.

### Damodaran’s piece

Aswath Damodaran published a long framework piece this week worth summarizing properly rather than skimming through it ([full piece here](https://aswathdamodaran.substack.com/p/ais-bar-mitzvah-moment-from-hope)).

His core argument: every revolutionary technology goes through four phases

- hope/hype,
- investing build-up,
- business building, and
- recalibration

and AI is now entering the business-building phase, where investors stop pricing on story alone and start demanding real evidence: revenue, margins, and defensible moats.
